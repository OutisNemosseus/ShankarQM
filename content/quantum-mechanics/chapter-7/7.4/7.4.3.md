# Shankar §7.4 Exercises 7.4.1–7.4.7

## Unified prerequisites: inputs not derived inside these exercises

### ① Orthonormal number basis
\[
\langle n'|n\rangle=\delta_{n'n},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots
\]

### ② Expectation value and variance
\[
\langle A\rangle=\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle,\qquad
(\Delta A)^2=\langle A^2\rangle-\langle A\rangle^2.
\]

### ③ Canonical commutator
\[
[X,P]=i\hbar I.
\]

### ④ Commutator identities
\[
[A,B]=AB-BA,
\]
\[
[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B,
\]
\[
[A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C].
\]

### ⑤ Harmonic-oscillator Hamiltonian
\[
H=\frac{P^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2X^2.
\]

### ⑥ Ladder operators
\[
a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}X+
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}P,
\]
\[
a^\dagger=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}X-
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}P.
\]

### ⑦ Ladder algebra
\[
[a,a^\dagger]=I,\qquad N=a^\dagger a,\qquad
N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle,
\]
\[
a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle,\qquad
a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle.
\]

### ⑧ \(X,P\) in ladder form
Define
\[
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}=m\omega\alpha.
\]
Then
\[
X=\alpha(a+a^\dagger),
\qquad
P=i\beta(a^\dagger-a).
\]

### ⑨ Useful identities
\[
aa^\dagger=N+I,
\]
\[
H=\hbar\omega\left(N+\frac12\right),
\]
\[
E_n=\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right).
\]

### ⑩ Time evolution
\[
|n,t\rangle=e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}|n\rangle.
\]

### ⑪ Ehrenfest theorem
For \(A\) with no explicit time dependence,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\langle A\rangle=
\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[H,A]\rangle.
\]
For the oscillator,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\langle X\rangle=\frac{\langle P\rangle}{m},
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}\langle P\rangle=-m\omega^2\langle X\rangle.
\]

### ⑫ Classical circular-orbit relations
\[
T=\frac12mv^2,\qquad
F_r=-\frac{dV}{dr},\qquad
\frac{mv^2}{r}=\left|\frac{dV}{dr}\right|.
\]

### ⑬ Poisson bracket
\[
\{f,g\}_{PB}
=
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p}
-
\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}.
\]

### ⑭ Poisson-bracket / commutator correspondence
If
\[
\{\omega,\lambda\}_{PB}=\chi,
\]
then one expects
\[
[\Omega,\Lambda]=i\hbar\,\widehat{\chi},
\]
subject to possible operator-ordering ambiguity.

# Exercise 7.4.3 — Virial Theorem

## Problem (English)

**The virial theorem in classical mechanics states that for a particle bound by a potential \(V(r)=ar^k\), the orbit-averaged kinetic and potential energies satisfy \(\overline T=c(k)\overline V\), where \(c(k)\) depends only on \(k\). Show that \(c(k)=k/2\) by considering a circular orbit. Using the previous exercise, show that for the oscillator (\(k=2\)), \(\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle\) in the quantum state \(|n\rangle\).**

## Solution

For
\[
V(r)=ar^k,
\]
\[
\left|\frac{dV}{dr}\right|=ak r^{k-1}.
\]
For a circular orbit,
\[
\frac{mv^2}{r}=ak r^{k-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
mv^2=ak r^k=kV.
\]
Since
\[
T=\frac12mv^2,
\]
\[
\boxed{T=\frac{k}{2}V}.
\]
For a circular orbit these are constant in time, so
\[
\boxed{c(k)=\frac{k}{2}}.
\]

For the quantum oscillator,
\[
T=\frac{P^2}{2m},
\qquad
V=\frac12m\omega^2X^2.
\]
Using 7.4.2,
\[
\langle T\rangle
=
\frac{1}{2m}
m\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right)
=
\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\left(n+\frac12\right),
\]
and
\[
\langle V\rangle
=
\frac12m\omega^2
\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}
\left(n+\frac12\right)
=
\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\left(n+\frac12\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle=\frac{E_n}{2}.
}
\]

## Logic chain
\[
\boxed{
⑫+V=ar^k
\Rightarrow
mv^2=kV
\Rightarrow
T=\frac{k}{2}V
\Rightarrow
c(k)=k/2
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
7.4.2+
T=P^2/2m+
V=m\omega^2X^2/2
\Rightarrow
\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle
}
\]
